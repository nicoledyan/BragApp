Performance reviews

How to remember your accomplishments at review time

Your best work should not disappear just because it happened six months before someone opened the review form.

Performance reviews often ask you to summarize months of work from memory. That is a bad match for how memory works. Recent projects feel larger, quiet contributions disappear, and the problems you prevented can be almost impossible to see afterward.

The fix is not a heroic search through old calendars and messages. It is a small, private record you update while the details are still fresh.

Record outcomes, not only completed tasks

A task says what you did. An accomplishment shows what changed because you did it. When you save a work win, capture the result alongside the action.

Task: “Updated the onboarding documentation.”

Accomplishment: “Reworked onboarding documentation around the questions new hires actually asked, cutting the number of setup escalations during the next cohort.”

You do not need a perfect metric for every entry. A useful outcome can be time saved, confusion removed, risk reduced, a decision unblocked, or a customer better served.

Save five kinds of evidence

  • Results: launches, improvements, milestones, and measurable changes.
  • Problem-solving: messy situations you clarified, fixed, or prevented.
  • Leadership: times you created direction, ownership, or calm—with or without a management title.
  • Collaboration: work you made easier or better for customers and teammates.
  • Growth: feedback you applied, skills you built, and responsibilities you grew into.

This creates a fuller account than a list of launches. It also captures the work that tends to be important but less visible.

Add context your future self will need

For each meaningful win, note the situation, what you contributed, and the effect. Add a number when one is available, but do not let a missing metric stop you from saving the entry.

“The project had stalled because three teams understood the decision differently. I wrote a one-page summary of the tradeoffs, brought the owners together, and got agreement on a path that kept the release date intact.”

Capture praise while you still have it

When a manager, teammate, or customer sends specific positive feedback, save the substance of it with the related accomplishment. Include who gave it and the context, but avoid copying confidential information into a tool that is not approved for it.

Feedback can help you see the effect of your work from another person’s perspective. It is especially useful when your contribution was supportive or behind the scenes.

Turn the record into a review

A few weeks before your review, group entries by theme rather than copying them in chronological order. Common themes include business results, customer impact, operational improvement, collaboration, and growth.

Choose the strongest examples under each theme, then connect them to the expectations of your role. Your saved details become the evidence; the review becomes the story of what that evidence shows.

Make it a weekly five-minute habit

Friday afternoon is a natural time to ask: What moved because I was here this week? Save one to three answers. A small weekly practice is more accurate and far less stressful than reconstructing a year at the last minute.

Your work is easier to explain when it is easier to remember
